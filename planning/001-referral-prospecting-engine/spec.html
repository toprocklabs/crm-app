Spec · Feature 001

Agentic referral-prospecting engine

Turn the sourcing map from a proximity viewer into an engine that finds the prospects our customers can warm-intro us to — and gets sharper at it over time.

Problem

Toprock grows on referrals from existing local, owner-operated customers. But finding who a customer could refer is manual and unsystematic. The map we built (/map) plots nearby businesses, but it treats every pin the same: it answers "what is near my customer?", not the question that actually drives revenue —

"Which nearby owner does my customer probably know, and which of those is actually worth winning — so I can ask for a warm intro?"

Two gaps follow from that. First, proximity alone is a weak proxy for "they know each other," and we do nothing to judge whether a sourced business is even a good fit. Second, nothing improves: every sweep is as naive as the first. We have the raw pieces — a trust graph, a geocoder, a scraper, a suggestion queue, an agent-run audit — but no engine that combines and learns from them.

The core model

Every prospect is judged on two multiplied factors. Either alone is noise; the product is signal.

Warmth

Does my customer actually know this owner? Physical co-location (same plaza > block > corridor > town) and affiliation / declared ties.

Fit

Is this a business worth winning? Resemblance to our existing customers (ICP look-alike) plus need/quality signals.

A strong referral prospect = high warmth × high fit. The engine scores both, ranks the product, explains the "why," drafts the intro, and learns which signals predict the deals we actually close.

Goals

  • Replace undifferentiated proximity pins with scored, ranked referral opportunities (warmth × fit) on the map and in the inbox.
  • Make it agentic but human-gated: a manually-triggered sweep that sources → qualifies → scores → drafts the intro; a person approves every action.
  • Give every score a glass-box rationale — the reviewer can always see why a prospect ranked where it did.
  • Establish a learning flywheel: turn approve / dismiss / won / lost into labels that refine a stored targeting profile over time — no ML infrastructure, fully inspectable.
  • Stay free / no-key (OSM, Overpass, Nominatim, Census), structured so paid providers can drop in later.

Non-goals

The boundary that keeps v1 honest:

  • No scheduled cadence yet. The sweep is manually triggered ("Scan this customer"). Graduating to a cron cadence is a deliberate later step, once the engine has earned trust.
  • No paid Google Maps / Places yet. We accept OSM's sparser coverage in exchange for zero keys; the geocoder + sourcing layer are already structured to swap Google in via env.
  • Not a refer-a-friend incentive/rewards program. This is relationship-driven prospecting, not a coupon engine.
  • No auto-sending. The agent drafts intros; the human sends them. Nothing leaves the building autonomously in v1.
  • Not every signal at once. Start with a few high-value signals; design the scorer to accept more as weighted inputs.

Success criteria

  • A manual scan of a known customer returns a ranked list with warmth + fit scores and a one-line rationale per prospect — not raw proximity pins.
  • The user judges the majority of top-ranked prospects "worth pursuing" (qualitative at first; trends up as the engine learns).
  • After several feedback cycles, ranking measurably shifts toward approved/won profiles — e.g. the rate at which repeatedly-dismissed categories appear near the top drops.
  • Each sweep completes within its declared token budget, with a recorded agent_runs row.
  • No regression to the existing approve / dismiss / map flows.

Open questions

  • First non-proximity signal? Brainstorm shortlist: declared digital ties (sites linking / IG tags / shared web-agency footer), complementary-trade affinity, and "just ask the customer." Which lands in v1?
  • Strongest success label — a won deal (precise, slow) vs. "became a real conversation" (faster, noisier)? Affects how quickly the engine learns.
  • Include "ask the customer" (owner-confirmed intro edges) in v1, or keep v1 fully inference-driven?
  • Model tiering + per-run budget ceiling the user is comfortable spending.
  • How tunable should weights be in the UI — read-only glass-box, or user-adjustable?
