Spec · Feature 003

Stripe payment mirror

Mirror Stripe payments into the CRM read-only, so every account shows what it has actually paid — and so the MRR we think we sold can be checked against the MRR Stripe is actually billing.

Problem

Stripe collects the money. The CRM tracks the relationship. Nothing connects them, so the system of record for accounts and deals has no idea whether a client has ever paid us a dollar. Answering "how much has this client paid?" means leaving the CRM, opening the Stripe dashboard, and searching by a name that may not match the account name.

Worse: the revenue numbers on our dashboard are guesses. "Won MRR" sums deals.valueCents — a figure a human typed into an opportunity. Stripe knows what is actually being billed every month. Those two numbers have never been compared, so nothing catches the gap.

That gap is where money leaks, quietly and for months:

  • A client signs a $250/mo care plan; the subscription is never created. We do the work for free.
  • A card fails, the subscription lapses, nobody notices. We keep doing the work.
  • A signed SOW says $3,500 implementation; only $1,500 is ever invoiced.

Plan 002 put signed agreements in the CRM. This plan closes the loop: what they agreed to pay now sits next to what they have paid.

Payment types

Two distinct kinds of money come in, and they reconcile against two different fields. Conflating them is the main way this feature could be built wrong. Both patterns are visible in the seven signed SOWs (proposal_creator/proposals/*/proposal.md, now mirrored in the proposals table):

Three details from the real contracts change how the comparison must work:

  • One-off fees arrive in installments, not one payment. Pacific Scuba's SOW is $750 total but bills as a $250 deposit for Phase 1, with "Phase 2 will be invoiced separately." Surf n Sport Chiro splits $300 into a $200 branding phase and a $100 build phase. So the honest one-off question is "how much of the contracted total have we collected so far?" — a progress bar with a remaining balance, not an equality check.
  • Not every client has a monthly plan. Pacific Scuba Repair and its intake SOW have no MONTHLY section at all. For those accounts expected MRR of zero is correct and must never be flagged as a missing subscription.
  • Maintenance often starts later than the signature. Garden of Eden bills "starting once the site is live"; Pacific Scuba's is "billed after build delivery." A signed SOW with no active subscription yet is frequently legitimate, so that state is a question to look at, not an error.
Fees are not a rounding error at these ticket sizes. Stripe takes roughly 2.9% + $0.30. On a $10/mo maintenance plan that is about 6% of the payment, and on a $15/mo plan about 4.5%. Gross collected and net received diverge enough to matter here, which is why fee capture is a Should rather than a nice-to-have.

Target workflow

1 · Link

Each CRM account is linked to its Stripe customer — auto-matched on email where confident, picked from a list otherwise.

2 · Mirror

Every charge and subscription syncs into the CRM automatically via webhook, with a one-time backfill for history.

3 · Read

The account page shows lifetime paid, the active subscription, and real MRR beside expected MRR.

Goals

  • Lifetime paid, per account. Open any account and see the total it has paid us, with the individual payments behind that number.
  • Recurring: real MRR vs. expected MRR. Stripe's active monthly billing for an account, shown next to the MRR recorded on its won opportunities, with the difference made obvious.
  • One-off: collected vs. contracted. How much of an account's contracted implementation fee has actually been collected, and what balance remains — since these are routinely billed as a deposit plus later phases.
  • Catch every payment regardless of how it was collected. Subscriptions, one-off invoices, and payment links all land in the same ledger — the billing process is inconsistent today and the mirror must not care.
  • Stay strictly read-only. The CRM observes Stripe. It never creates, charges, refunds, or modifies anything.
  • Attribute or surface. A payment that can't be matched to an account is visibly unlinked, never silently dropped.

Non-goals

  • No writing to Stripe. No creating invoices, subscriptions, or charges from the CRM. This is deliberate for v1 — see plan 003's phasing for where that could go later.
  • No dunning or collections workflow. Overdue-invoice tracking is a natural follow-on, but is out of scope here; only the data needed for it gets synced.
  • Not a Stripe dashboard replacement. Disputes, payouts, fee reporting, and balance management stay in Stripe. Deep links from the CRM cover those.
  • No customer-facing billing portal. Clients don't log into the CRM.
  • Not an accounting system. Gross amounts collected, not revenue recognition, tax treatment, or bookkeeping export.

Success criteria

  • Opening any linked account answers "how much have they paid us?" without leaving the CRM.
  • Every historical Stripe charge appears in the ledger exactly once, and the sum reconciles against the Stripe dashboard total.
  • A payment that clears in Stripe shows up on the account within seconds, without anyone running a script.
  • At least one real discrepancy between expected and actual MRR is surfaced on first run — or we confirm with evidence there are none.
  • No Stripe customer with payment history is left unattributed and invisible.
  • The API key in the environment cannot create a charge, verified by attempting one.

Open questions

The one that most affects correctness: do any clients pay outside Stripe — check, ACH transfer, Venmo, cash? If so, "lifetime paid" sourced purely from Stripe is wrong for those accounts, and R15 (manual payment entry) moves from Could to Must.
  • How far back should the backfill go — all history, or a cutoff date?
  • Is there more than one Stripe account (e.g. a legacy or personal account) that also holds client payments?
  • Should a refunded payment reduce lifetime paid outright, or show as a separate credited figure?
  • Expected MRR source: sum of won opportunities only, or should proposal/negotiation stages count toward a forecast column too?
  • How long after signing should a missing maintenance subscription stop being "not started yet" and start reading as a problem? Two SOWs defer billing to launch, so a fixed grace period is a guess until you name one.
  • Do the phase-two amounts get invoiced when the phase ships, or is the full contracted total billed up front in practice? Determines whether a remaining balance is a red flag or just normal work-in-progress.

Answered by reading the contracts:

  • Do any subscriptions bill annually? — All seven SOWs price maintenance monthly; none are annual. ToS §4.2 still permits annual billing, so the monthly normalization stays in the design as cheap insurance, but it has no effect on today's data.
  • Are one-off fees a single payment? — No. Deposits and phased billing are the norm (ToS §4.1 allows requiring a deposit), which is why R6b treats one-off as progress rather than equality.
