## 1. Brandon's vision & priorities

*Brandon has a big vision he's been circling for a while but hasn't pulled the trigger on — the website has sat far down his priority list, but it keeps resurfacing.*

- What he needs near-term: a **nice landing page** — "an online business card" — and a clean way to **put out content** about what he's seeing and doing.
- Longer term: build in **paid programs / paid courses**, lean into **social media + YouTube**, create interest there and **funnel people into the website**, then into higher-ticket one-on-one work.
- Content vision: blog posts with an **embedded YouTube video + a downloadable PDF** the person can keep.
- When asked to rank gut-first: **rebrand first**, then build the website, then a platform to "dump content into" within a cohesive brand — all while keeping the one-on-one care strong.

## 2. The rebrand: Surf N Sport → Vital Alignments

*The current name was created in chiropractic school and now "feels like a past version of me." Brandon has tinkered with rebranding across two prior periods without launching it.*

- **Why move off "Surf N Sport Chiropractic":** it's too niche. He started wanting to work with surfers/athletes — and still does — but he also serves "moms, dads, grandmas." He wants something more encompassing of his philosophy.
- **The philosophy driving "Vital Alignment(s)":** "It's not the adjustment on the table that makes the alignment in their life. It's adjusting a belief, a mindset, their thoughts… it could be the body, emotions, or spirit." The new brand keeps the root but has grown/evolved.
- **Scope of the rebrand:** "everything across the board" — logo, colors, feel. Everything he's built so far is themed for Surf N Sport (beach feel: sand tones, water tones, light tan, light blue, dark blue, dark brown). He's open to carrying those color schemes forward or changing them.
- Self-aware tension: he's put a lot of meaning into it but worries about **overanalyzing** — "stop thinking about it, just do."

## 3. Current web & brand state

*Brandon already went through one branding/website cycle with an outside designer, and it's the baseline he wants to elevate from.*

- Started on **WordPress**; a patient (during his time with Dr. Bueller) told him it "looks like a Word document you just published." The version he showed Top Rock is an updated layer on top of that.
- He hired a website designer/builder, **Hayes**, who built much of it and helped a lot with his branding (they did a full mood-board process — "turned out really well, but it was a long process").
- For the rebrand he tried to re-engage Hayes directly, but was handed to her second-in-command, **Maggie** — "nothing against Maggie," but it fell through because he wanted to work with Hayes herself.
- He hasn't evaluated other tools/options — "it's been working, but I want to elevate."

## 4. How clients find him today

*Brandon walked through his current acquisition path — almost entirely organic.*

- Biggest channel by far: **word of mouth / referrals.**
- From there people **track him down via Instagram**, or grab a **brochure / flyer** that has his website on it.
- The **website funnels them to scheduling** — they reach out by phone to book, or he manually enters them into **Jane App** (his EMR/scheduling/billing).

## 5. Top Rock's approach & the AI advantage

*Top Rock framed who they are and why their model fits a rebrand like this.*

- **Not a traditional branding/marketing agency.** Both founders are technical (computer science / mechanical engineering backgrounds) who ended up in the business/sales world — they care about business, know how to ask questions, and merge the technical + people sides. They were upfront: "we're not branding experts."
- **The AI advantage = speed of iteration.** Instead of one direction + a multi-week mood board, they can have a 30-minute conversation about Brandon's philosophy, feed it to AI, and generate **five different full mockup directions** he can actually click through — then iterate fast toward the one that resonates.
- They showed a live example: an **epoxy floor-coating B2B client** who wanted an "Apple showroom vibe" — five distinct mockups (showroom/white, dark/auto, tech-heavy, space-like) that each "feel extremely different but feel real."
- Other examples: a **high-school football recruiting site** (roster overviews for college scouts) and their own **custom CRM** (a Salesforce-style deal tracker they built themselves with AI, used to manage clients like "Scuba Dive Utah").
- **Custom > template:** unlike Jane App or School.com — built once and everyone conforms to their format/branding — AI lets Top Rock build something customized to the individual, and reskin later as trends change.

## 6. Phased delivery

*To keep it manageable (Brandon noted he gets overwhelmed by everything he wants to do), the work breaks into sections.*

- **Phase 1:** brand + website.
- **Phase 2:** a content section.
- **Phase 3:** courses, or possibly a forum/community (Brandon likes that forums are "coming back").
- Brandon's framing: he wants an **integrated team around him** — Top Rock as one flexible, high-value piece that plugs into each client's needs; bring in specialists (e.g. a branding firm, YouTube help) where it makes sense.

## 7. Pricing context

*Cost framing came up by way of what Brandon paid before.*

- Brandon paid Hayes roughly **~$3,000** for his initial brand/design (in separate payments).
- Top Rock noted a full brand typically runs **$5,000–$10,000** in the market — and that they charge nowhere near that because most of their cost is AI tokens, not full-time headcount.
- They flagged there'd be a **small down payment** in the proposal just to cover the AI cost of building everything out.
- Brandon: *"Well, I'm in."* — happy to proceed to a written proposal.
